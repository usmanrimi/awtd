The problem

Africa's education system and its employers are not speaking. Nigeria's TVET enrolment sits below 10% of tertiary education, while employers report graduates who lack hands-on skills, safety culture, and work-readiness. The bridge between academic training and real employment is broken.

<10% Nigeria TVET share of tertiary enrolment
70% AWTD hands-on training ratio
6 Months to job-readiness
0 Safety incident tolerance

The Status Quo vs. The AWTD Blueprint

Select any operational dimension below to compare conventional vocational schooling against AWTD's employer-led training blueprint.

The Status Quo

Conventional TVET
  • Years-long, school-like and theoretical programmes
  • Isolated from industry, with no employer co-design
  • Assessment by written exam only
  • Static curricula that don't reflect market demand
  • TVETs perceived as second-tier and under-resourced
  • Long time-to-productivity after hiring

The AWTD Blueprint

Employer-Verified
  • Months-long, short-cycle and stackable modules
  • Employers co-design competencies from day one
  • Performance-based portfolios and certifications
  • Built for rapid iteration and replication
  • Workshop-first: hands-on before theory
  • Paid apprenticeships with employer mentors
Practical Readiness

Workshop-First Before Theory

Employers consistently report that academic graduates require months of post-hire retraining. AWTD reverses the sequence: 70% of instruction takes place on real tools under strict workplace safety protocols.

Closing the technician gap

Each pillar of the AWTD Blueprint directly resolves a structural bottleneck identified by industrial and infrastructure employers.
